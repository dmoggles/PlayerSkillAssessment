---
title: Training sessions
summary: Plan team sessions around what your players are working on, then record who came.
---
Open **Sessions** to plan a team training session. TapLine IDP builds it around the skills your players are working on, then you adjust it, print it, and record how it went.

## Planning a session

Press **Plan a session**, then choose:

- **Date and start time.**
- **Plan:** the default for a length of 45, 60, 75 or 90 minutes, or one of your team's templates (see **Templates** below). The form shows how long each part will take.
- **Focus:** up to three skills. The suggestions come from the priorities you've confirmed in **Development**, with how many players share each one. The first skill counts most.
- **Players expected:** everyone in the squad to start with; untick anyone you know won't be there.
- **Levels:**
  - **One version for everyone:** each drill runs at the squad's level on the focus skill.
  - **Level groups:** the squad splits into 2 or 3 groups by their rating on the focus skill. The technical and opposed parts run in the groups, each at its own version of the same drill; the warm-up, small-sided game and cool-down run with everyone together.

Press **Plan the session**. By default the session is split into a warm-up, a technical practice, an opposed practice, a small-sided game and a cool-down; a template can have other parts. Each part gets a drill for that part of a session, chosen for:

- how well it trains the focus;
- whether it suits the number of players, and fits the time;
- your likes, and other coaches' votes;
- variety: drills from your last few sessions are less likely;
- drills players already have in their own plans.

Each block says why its drill was chosen. Where no drill in the library fits, the block is left for your own activity, such as a cool-down jog and stretches.

Warm-ups and cool-downs don't have to train the focus: a general warm-up fits any session, though one that trains the focus is preferred. The FIFA 11+ injury-prevention warm-ups take 15–20 minutes to do properly. When one is chosen, the technical and opposed practices are a few minutes shorter to make room, so the session still ends on time. Each keeps at least 5 minutes; a session with neither keeps the warm-up to its planned time.

## Templates

A template is your own breakdown of a session: its length, and how many minutes each part takes. Press **Templates** in **Sessions** to see your team's templates, and **New template** to add one:

- **Name:** for example *Match prep*.
- **Length:** 15 to 150 minutes.
- **Minutes for each part:** warm-up, technical, opposed, small-sided game, full squad game and cool-down. Leave a part empty to leave it out. The parts must add up to the length before you can save.

A **full squad game** is a match with everyone together, even when the session runs in level groups. The library has no full squad game drills yet, so the block is left for you to describe.

Choose a template under **Plan** when you plan a session. Everything else works the same: the focus, the players, one version or level groups, editing and re-planning. A session keeps the breakdown it was planned with, so editing or deleting a template doesn't change sessions already planned. Templates are shared by every coach in the team.

## Changing it

- **Edit** a block to change its minutes, choose another drill (suggestions for that part of the session first, or any drill), change a group's version, or replace the drill with your own activity.
- **+ Add a block** for your own activity in any part of the session.
- **Change groups** to move players between level groups. Each group's version restarts at its level.
- **Re-plan** plans the session again. Blocks you changed or added are kept, marked *Your choice*.

Every coach in the team can see and change the team's sessions.

## Printing

**Print** gives you a sheet with the times, drills and each group's version, to take to training. Open a drill to see its diagram, setup and coaching points.

## Afterwards

- **Attendance:** tick who came. Before the session, ticking who's expected updates the groups.
- **Add note** on a block to say how it went, and write a few lines under **How did it go?** for the whole session.

> **Tip:** Which sessions a player was expected at and came to is part of what's recorded about them, so it's included when an owner exports a player's data, and removed when a player is deleted.
