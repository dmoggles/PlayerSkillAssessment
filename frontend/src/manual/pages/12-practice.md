title: Practice scenarios
summary: Game situations players solve on their phone, with feedback on every answer, and how they're going.
---
Practice scenarios are game situations frozen on a pitch. The player drags themselves to where they'd stand, presses **Check**, and sees how good the spot was and why. They teach decisions, not speed: there are no timers, and no scores against anyone else. Players do them on their phone at home, from a link you give them.

The first topic is **off-ball defending**, in five stages:

1. **Goal-side basics:** between your attacker and the goal, close when the ball is near them and dropped off when it's far.
2. **The ball moves:** the ball is passed, and the player moves again.
3. **Press or cover:** one defender presses the ball, the other covers behind.
4. **Two attackers:** read who the danger is. A free attacker nearer goal than the ball is yours to pick up; one level with the ball or further away isn't yet, so stay goal-side and cover the teammate pressing the ball.
5. **Just lost the ball:** situations from real games, with shirt numbers. The player solves the same moment as each defender in turn, starting from where that player actually was, so they learn their partners' jobs as well as their own.

Each scenario is for a format (7v7 to start with) and a range of age groups. More topics will follow, such as moving to receive the ball.

## Trying them yourself

**Settings → Practice scenarios → Browse scenarios** lists every scenario, for your team's age group or all of them. **Preview** plays one as a player would; nothing is saved.

## Random situations

Stages 1 to 4 can also make new situations at random, so there's always more to practise:
- they're built from the stage's ideas, such as goal-side of the free attacker, or covering a teammate who presses;
- each is scored and gives clues like the hand-made ones;
- they can be on a 7v7, 9v9 or 11v11 pitch. The distances stay the same; only the pitch changes.

**Random situation** on a stage plays one; **Another one** at the end makes the next. Choose the pitch under **Random situations on**. Stage 5's situations come from real games, so they're never random.

## Setting a player practice

Open the player in **Player Data → Practice**.

- **Give a link** makes the player's practice link. Send it to the player or a parent: anyone with it can practise as that player, so don't share it more widely. It works for 30 days. **New link** replaces it, and **Stop the link** stops it at once. The link is shown only when you make it.
- **Set practice:** choose a whole stage, one scenario, or **random situations** from a stage (how many, and on which pitch), and a date to do it by if you like. **Remove** takes it off their list; what they've already done is kept.

## What the player sees

Their first name and the practice you set, with how many they've done. Random practice has a **Start** button that makes each situation as they go. A stage you've set also has **New situation**, so they can keep going after its scenarios. In each scenario they drag their player (or tap where they'd stand) and press **Check**. They aren't told which attacker is theirs: working that out is part of it.

- They see **Great position**, **Good** or **Not quite**, and the points (up to 100).
- When it isn't the best spot, they get a clue and **Try again**:
  - the first clue is a question about the idea, such as *If they got the ball now, could they run straight at goal?*;
  - the second is a plainer pointer, such as *Stand on the line from the attacker to the middle of your goal*. For goal-side and ball-side, the lines to goal come on too;
  - after a third miss, a ghost shows the best spot and why it's best.

  A player who's stuck can skip a step.
- in scenarios with more than one step, the ball and players move and they position themselves again;
- **Show lines to goal** draws lines from the ball and each attacker to the goal, to make goal-side easier to see. It starts on in stage 1.

At the end they get one to three stars, from their first try at each step.

## How it's going

**Player Data → Practice** shows:

- each piece of practice, with how many scenarios are done and the stars for each; press a scenario to preview it;
- the tries each week for the last eight weeks, with the average points on a first try;
- **Missed most lately:** what the player's last 50 tries got wrong most, such as *Not goal-side*. It's a good place to start in your next session;
- average points for each concept, such as goal-side or covering.

> **Tip:** This is practice, not assessment. It's kept apart from your ratings and never changes them.

## What's kept

For each try: the scenario and step, where the player put themselves, the points, what it missed and how long it took. Nothing else: no free text, device or location. It's included when an owner exports the player's data, and deleted with the player.
